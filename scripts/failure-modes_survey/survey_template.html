What counts as a failure paper here

The atlas tags a paper failure-modes when characterizing a way models break is its primary or notable contribution — naming a failure class, taxonomizing one, or demonstrating one empirically. Hallucination analyses, sycophancy, reward hacking, jailbreaks and prompt injection, tool-use breakdowns, error compounding across agent loops, deception, sandbagging, unfaithful reasoning, red-team studies. A defense paper is a member when it diagnoses what it defends against — Spotlighting maps the injection surface before it patches it, so it is in; a training method that merely reports fewer errors is out. A model, an agent framework, or a capability paper that exhibits errors in passing is out. That rubric yields @@N@@ papers, every one with a full explainer on this site; this page is generated from those explainers.

The corpus runs @@YEAR_MIN@@–@@YEAR_MAX@@ but is sharply modern: @@N_SINCE_2023@@ of @@N@@ papers (@@PCT_SINCE_2023@@%) are from 2023 or later, with the single largest year being @@N_2024@@ papers in 2024. The pre-LLM tail is deliberate and thin — @@N_ERA_2018@@ papers through 2018, @@N_ERA_201920@@ in 2019–20 — because the vocabulary has a lineage worth keeping: Concrete Problems in AI Safety named reward hacking and scalable oversight in @@YEAR_MIN@@ with @@Q_CP_MODES@@ failure modes and @@Q_CP_EXPERIMENTS@@ experiments, and the Goodhart taxonomy formalized proxy failure before any language model could exhibit it. What the corpus is not is a census: it over-represents what the atlas covers deeply — text-model and agent failures in English, published in the last few years — and it holds papers, not incidents, so failure classes that live in postmortems and bug trackers rather than in preprints are absent by construction.

The prior surveys are inside the corpus — and they all sort by symptom

This survey does not need to reach outside itself to find its prior work. @@N_EVD_SYNTH@@ of the @@N@@ papers are themselves syntheses — their evidence mode is corpus-synthesis, meaning they organize other people's results rather than producing their own. They are the field's existing maps: Concrete Problems (@@YEAR_MIN@@), the hallucination survey, its multimodal sequel, RLHF's open problems, the agentic-harms vocabulary, the manipulation framework, the deception review, a sycophancy survey, and @@YEAR_MAX@@'s model-or-harness attribution taxonomy. Widen the frame past this corpus and the same shelf holds the broad LLM-risk taxonomies — Weidinger et al.'s risk-area taxonomy, the MIT AI Risk Repository's causal-plus-domain database, the industry vulnerability lists — and their organizing move is identical.

Every one of them sorts failures by symptom: what went wrong, in what capability, to whom. None of them sorts by how the result was obtained. That omission is not cosmetic. Across this corpus, papers that share a symptom routinely differ by an order of magnitude in what their evidence supports, because one ran a model on ordinary inputs and another wrote a fictional corporate crisis designed to produce exactly the behaviour it then reports. Sorting by symptom hides that difference; the taxonomies above have no column for it.

The other thing a symptom taxonomy hides is its own evidential base. Of the @@N_EVD_SYNTH@@ syntheses here, all belong to the not-elicited class — @@N_ELI_NONE@@ papers in the corpus run no experiment at all — and the eval read on several is unkind: the agentic-harms paper lists @@Q_AH_AUTHORS@@ authors and @@Q_AH_MEASURE@@ measurements; the sycophancy survey carries @@Q_SYC_ORIGINAL@@ original measurements and @@Q_SYC_MISATTR@@ misattributed citations. A taxonomy that never touches a model can still be the most-cited object in its subfield — Concrete Problems is proof — but it licenses vocabulary, not rates. Distinguishing the two is what the axis below is for.

// the load-bearing axis

Elicitation: how much of the failure did you build?

Ask of any result in this corpus: what did the researcher have to construct before the failure showed up? The answers form a short ladder, and it is not a ladder of severity or of rigor. It is a ladder of apparatus. At one end, the model was doing something ordinary and the failure was already there. At the other, the failure had to be installed by fine-tuning. In between sit the prompt, the search over prompts, and the staged world.

The cut that turns out to matter is not where a paper sits on that ladder but which object it manufactured. On the first three rungs the researcher produced (or simply received) an input, and whatever the model then did is still an output someone can read. On the next two the researcher built the context the model reasons in, or the weights it reasons with, and the output it produces afterwards is by construction indistinguishable from an ordinary one. Finding 02 is that distinction, measured.

// definition

Elicitation — the class of apparatus a paper needed to produce the failure it reports. Spontaneous: it appeared under ordinary use of the system, with nothing adversarial constructed. Prompted: a hand-written input triggered it. Optimized: a gradient, a search, or an attacker model produced the input. Constructed-scenario: a whole situation was staged — roles, stakes, and a temptation. Trained-in: the failure was installed by fine-tuning, poisoning, or an RL run. Not-elicited: nothing was run; the paper is a taxonomy, a survey, or a proof.

The reason this belongs at the front of a failure survey rather than in its methods appendix is that elicitation bounds the claim. A spontaneous result — Lost in the Middle's @@Q_LITM_GAP@@ swing depending on where the evidence sits in the context window — licenses a statement about ordinary use. finding 06

Nobody measures the confound, and everybody who did found it

@@CHART_AWARENESS@@

The last facet is the one that makes finding 02 dangerous. If a result is produced by staging a situation, its validity depends on the model treating the situation as real. @@N_AWR_TESTED@@ of @@N@@ papers test that assumption — @@PCT_AWR_TESTED_OF_APPLICABLE@@% of the @@N_AWR_APPLICABLE@@ where it is even applicable. That last count needs its caveat attached: the facet has no value for a null result, so a paper that tested and found nothing would be recorded as not-tested. The zero is therefore a fact about a vocabulary that was drafted with a fourth slot and trimmed when nothing filled it, not an independent measurement — which is exactly why it belongs in the prose, where it can be argued, rather than sitting in an enum where it would read as settled. It is not evidence that a null result is impossible — it is evidence that the field has @@N_AWR_TESTED@@ data points and they all lean one way.

The clearest of them undercuts its own paper's headline. That defense is plausible for a suffix and much weaker for a staged agentic environment; @@N_NOTTESTED_SCEN@@ constructed-scenario papers and @@N_NOTTESTED_TRAIN@@ trained-in papers also went unchecked. The one encouraging pattern in the data cuts against the easy story: the rung most exposed to the confound is also the rung most likely to test it — @@N_AWR_SCEN@@ of the @@N_SCEN_APPLICABLE@@ applicable constructed-scenario papers checked (@@PCT_SCEN_TESTED@@%), against @@N_NOTTESTED_PROMPT@@ of @@N_PROMPT_APPLICABLE@@ unchecked among prompted ones. The people staging worlds know what they are risking. Everyone else has decided the question does not apply to them, and has not checked.

finding 07

The failures we study are the ones that embarrass the principals

@@CHART_HARMWAVES@@

Who bears the cost, by era. The user and the operator dominate throughout; third parties are the thinnest slice in the corpus.

Sort by who eats the cost and the corpus is a study of the two parties in the transaction. @@N_HRM_PRINCIPALS@@ of @@N@@ papers (@@PCT_HRM_PRINCIPALS@@%) describe a failure whose cost lands on the user (@@N_HRM_USER@@) or the operator (@@N_HRM_OPERATOR@@) — the person holding the chat window and the company that deployed it. Bystanders get @@N_HRM_BYSTANDERS@@ (@@PCT_HRM_BYSTANDERS@@%), and the split inside that is telling: society at @@N_HRM_SOCIETY@@ — mostly the jailbreak literature, where the harm is diffuse and hypothesized — against a named third party at just @@N_HRM_THIRD@@. That is the whole measurement-integrity kingdom plus JudgeDeceiver, and it includes some of the most consequential results here: Style Over Substance finds a response with minor factual errors scoring @@Q_STYLE_HI@@ Elo against @@Q_STYLE_LO@@ for one that is correct but short; the ABC checklist finds @@Q_ABC_TASK@@ audited agent benchmarks violate task validity and @@Q_ABC_REPORT@@ have reporting limitations. Five things no single paper shows

These were traced through the corpus's internal lineage graph — @@N_EDGES@@ parent edges among the @@N@@ papers, @@N_EDGES_CROSS@@ of them crossing kingdoms, with @@N_ROOTS@@ papers having no in-corpus parent — read against the full text of every explainer. Each is a connection you cannot see from inside any one paper.

connection 01 · the injection that keeps coming back

The most-descended paper in the corpus is a demo with no success rates, and years of defenses have not closed it

Indirect Prompt Injection is tied for the most-descended node here — @@N_HUB_IPI@@ in-corpus children — and it reports @@Q_IPI_RATES@@ success rates. It is a case demonstration: working exploits across threat categories on deployed systems, no table. What it produced was a research programme. One of its parents, PromptInject, had already measured @@Q_PI_HIJACK@@ goal hijacking on text-davinci-002; its children measured the surface (OPI, HouYi, Neural Exec), instrumented it inside agents (InjecAgent, AgentDojo), and defended it — @@N_DEFENDS_EDGES@@ of the corpus's lineage edges are explicit defends-against links, aimed at @@N_DEFENDS_TARGETS@@ targets, and @@N_DEFENDS_AT_IPI@@ of them point at this one paper: StruQ, Spotlighting, and the Instruction Hierarchy.

Those defenses worked. StruQ drove a completion attack from @@Q_STRUQ_HI@@ to @@Q_STRUQ_LO@@; Spotlighting took success below @@Q_SPOT_LO@@. And then the same family's @@YEAR_MAX@@ descendant, ASPI, takes o3 from @@Q_ASPI_O3_LO@@ to @@Q_ASPI_O3_HI@@ and Kimi K2.5 to @@Q_ASPI_KIMI_HI@@ with a new injection shape. StruQ's own numbers contain the warning: it reports a residual @@Q_STRUQ_GCG@@ under GCG-style optimized attack, in the same paper as its headline zero. The pattern the lineage graph makes visible is that every defense in this family is scoped to an attack shape, and the attack shape is the cheapest thing in the system to change.

connection 02 · the filter that was already broken

One of the corpus's @@N_MIT_EFFECTIVE@@ effective mitigations was defeated by a sibling paper posted a month later, and the graph shows they never met

GCG is the corpus's other most-descended node (@@N_HUB_GCG@@ children). One of its children, Baseline Defenses, is one of only @@N_MIT_EFFECTIVE@@ papers here classified effective-mitigation: a perplexity filter drives GCG suffixes to a @@Q_BASE_PPL@@ pass rate, because the suffixes are gibberish and gibberish has high perplexity. Another child, AutoDAN, responds to the same parent by generating readable jailbreaks — @@Q_AUTODAN_ASR@@ attack success against GCG's @@Q_AUTODAN_GCG@@ on Llama2-7b-chat — and reports the filter's verdict directly: under a perplexity filter, GCG falls to @@Q_AUTODAN_FILTER@@ while AutoDAN is unchanged. The two preprints are @@MONTHS_BD_AUTODAN@@ month apart — the tree above labels them ’23 and ’24 because AutoDAN's year is its ICLR appearance, not its posting date.

Neither paper cites the other in this corpus; they are siblings, not a dialogue. Read together they say something neither says alone: the corpus's "effective" label is doing much less work than it looks like. A mitigation is only effective against the attack distribution its evaluators had, and in this case a contemporaneous paper had already moved the distribution. The same shape recurs across the adversarial-breach kingdom, which is where @@N_KING_F_FAIL@@ of the corpus's @@N_MIT_FAIL@@ defenses-fail verdicts live (@@PCT_KING_F_FAIL_SHARE@@%) — and where MHJ found human red-teamers reaching @@Q_MHJ_HUMAN@@ attack success on a defended model that an automated ensemble scored at @@Q_MHJ_AUTO@@, and J2 found an LLM red-teamer at @@Q_J2_ASR@@ against human teamers' @@Q_J2_HUMAN@@.

connection 03 · one kingdom is doing all the epistemics

Every paper that checks whether the model knows it is being tested belongs to the same @@N_KING_AWR_TOTAL@@-paper kingdom

All @@N_AWR_TESTED@@ papers that measured evaluation awareness sit in @@N_AWR_KING_LABEL@@ — @@N_AWR_TESTED@@ of that kingdom's @@N_KING_AWR_TOTAL@@ members. The other @@N_OTHER_KINGDOM_PAPERS@@ papers in the corpus, across the other @@N_KINGDOMS_OTHER@@ kingdoms, contain not one such measurement. And the lineage graph shows why: this kingdom is a single tight cluster, not @@N_KING_AWR_TOTAL@@ independent efforts. In-context Scheming has @@N_HUB_ICS@@ in-corpus children and Unfaithful CoT another @@N_HUB_UNFAITHFUL@@; CoT Obfuscation has @@N_HUB_OBFUSCATION@@, and Agentic Misalignment descends from @@N_AM_PARENTS@@ of the others at once.

The reason is structural rather than cultural. This is the only kingdom whose subject matter is the gap between what a model does and what it appears to do — so a researcher studying monitor evasion has no choice but to ask what the subject believes. Everywhere else the question is optional, and it is skipped. That is a discipline-shaped hole: the papers most in need of the check are the constructed-scenario papers in other kingdoms — the agentic sandboxes of ToolEmu, PrivacyLens, AgentDAM and @@YEAR_MAX@@'s escalation benchmarks — every one of which stages a fictional world and scores what the model does inside it, and none of which reports whether the model believed it.

connection 04 · the same model, a worse wrapper

The agentic literature keeps rediscovering that the failure was added by the scaffold, not the model

Three lines of work converge on one claim without sharing a family. BrowserART runs the identical model in two wrappers and finds harmful-request compliance rising from @@Q_BART_CHAT@@ in chat to @@Q_BART_AGENT@@ as an agent — the refusal training is intact, and the scaffold routes around it. InjecAgent and AgentDojo find that the injection surface only exists once a model is given tools and untrusted content in the same context. And Model or Harness, the corpus's newest synthesis, makes the claim its title: a large share of what gets scored as model failure is attributable to the components around it.

The facet data agrees. The agent-trajectory locus holds @@N_LOC_TRAJECTORY@@ papers and eval-harness another @@N_LOC_HARNESS@@ — together @@PCT_LOC_TRAJECTORY@@% plus @@PCT_LOC_HARNESS@@% of the corpus locating the failure outside the weights entirely. This matters for how the field's headline numbers should be read: a benchmark score for "agentic safety" is a score for a model and a scaffold and a harness, and the corpus contains @@N_KING_H@@ papers in the measurement-integrity kingdom arguing that the last two are not held fixed between studies. MCP-Atlas puts a number on the split from the other direction: @@Q_MCP_COGNITIVE@@ of its observed failures are cognitive rather than mechanical — which is only meaningful because someone separated the two.

connection 05 · the proofs

The corpus's only formal results all argue that the failure class cannot be engineered away

@@N_EVD_PROOF@@ papers here offer a proof rather than a measurement, and they arrive at versions of the same uncomfortable conclusion. The Goodhart taxonomy (2018) enumerates the mechanisms by which optimizing a proxy diverges from the goal. Defining and Characterizing Reward Hacking proves it: on any policy set containing an open subset, unhackable non-trivial reward pairs must be equivalent — there is no clever proxy that is both different from the true reward and safe to maximize. Hallucination is Inevitable proves an @@Q_HALLU_INEV@@ for all computable language models. And Why Language Models Hallucinate supplies the mechanism for why nobody fixes it: @@Q_WHY_BINARY@@ surveyed benchmarks grade purely binary, so guessing beats abstaining and the training signal rewards confident error.

Set that against the mitigation data and the field's shape makes sense. @@PCT_MIT_NOTHING@@% of papers leave you with no demonstrated defense not because the field is careless but because a nontrivial share of these failures are properties of the setup — the objective, the grading scheme, the computability of the model class — rather than defects in an implementation. The corollary is uncomfortable for the largest kingdom: TruthfulQA's best model at @@Q_TQA_BEST@@ truthful against @@Q_TQA_HUMAN@@ for humans, in 2021, was never going to be closed by better prompting. Where a failure is provably structural, the useful question stops being "how do we stop it" and becomes "how do we detect it" — which brings the survey back to finding 02, and to the @@N_DEEP_OUTPUT@@ deep-rung failures visible from the output.

How this was built, and what to distrust

Corpus. The @@N@@ papers are the atlas's failure-modes survey tag — the same set you see ringed on the timeline map. Membership follows the tag rubric (characterizing a failure as the primary or notable contribution), applied when each paper joined the atlas. The corpus over-represents English-language text and agent failures from 2023–@@YEAR_MAX@@ (@@N_SINCE_2023@@ of @@N@@ papers) and treats the pre-LLM safety literature as ancestry; era trends should be read as trends within this kind of corpus, not a census of everything that has ever been called an AI failure. It is also a corpus of papers: failure classes that live in incident reports, postmortems, and vulnerability disclosures are absent by form, not by judgment.

Classification. Each paper was classified along the @@N_FACETS@@ facets and placed at one tree address by reading its atlas explainer — the same explainer a reader can click through to from every chip and table row above — with the classification recorded in data/failure-modes-taxonomy.json alongside a written reason for each of the load-bearing calls (elicitation, eval_awareness, locus, detectability, mitigation_status, harm_bearer, and placement). @@N_CONF_HIGH@@ of @@N@@ records carry a high-confidence flag and @@N_CONF_MED@@ are medium; treat individual facet calls on the medium ones as softer than the aggregate distributions.

On the missing enum value. The eval_awareness facet has @@N_AWR_VALUES@@ values. An earlier draft carried a fourth slot for "tested, no effect found" — and nothing filled it, because no paper in this corpus reports a null evaluation-awareness result. A closed vocabulary with a permanently empty slot invites a reader to wonder what is missing there, so the slot was removed and the absence written into finding 06 instead, where it can be argued rather than implied. The same principle applies to any count on this page that reads as zero: @@N_DEEP_OUTPUT@@ of @@N_DEEP_ELI@@ deep-rung failures visible from output and @@N_KING_F_SPONTANEOUS@@ spontaneous failures in the adversarial-breach kingdom are computed, not asserted.

Numbers. Every count, share and ratio above is computed at build time from the classification file by scripts/failure-modes_survey/stats_tokens.py and substituted into the prose as a token; none is typed by hand. The figures quoted from individual papers (@@Q_GCG_ASR@@, @@Q_AM_REAL@@, @@Q_SA_HELDOUT@@ and the rest) are likewise extracted from each record's key_number field by an anchored pattern that fails the build if the record changes — so re-classifying a paper cannot silently leave a stale quotation on the page.

Known soft spots. (1) elicitation records the apparatus behind the paper's headline result; several papers run more than one kind of experiment, and a multi-label version of this axis would be more faithful and less legible. (2) mitigation_status records what the paper itself demonstrates — a failure with an excellent defense published elsewhere still reads as none-proposed here, which is why the @@N_MIT_EFFECTIVE@@-in-@@N@@ figure should be read as "how often a failure paper closes its own loop", not "how often the field has a fix". (3) detectability asks what you would need in a deployment you control; a determined operator with full logging can see more than the label implies. (4) The earliest eras hold only @@N_ERA_2018@@ and @@N_ERA_201920@@ papers, which makes the leftmost bars of every trend chart indicative, not precise. (5) eval_awareness is a facet about the literature, not about models: n-a covers @@N_AWR_NA@@ papers where no model is measured or the systems studied predate any capacity to represent being evaluated, and the boundary between n-a and not-tested moves as models get more situationally aware.

What review changed. This page went through three adversarial review rounds against the taxonomy and the explainers, and the defects that came out of them fall into four classes. (1) Inverted lineage edges. Five edges asserted ancestry from a paper that postdated its child — one descends-from, two generalizes, two responds-to — and two of those also carried the correct reverse edge, so the graph held contradictory cycles. All five are removed. test_data_integrity.py now checks strictly that a descends-from, generalizes or formalizes parent precedes its child; responds-to is exempt rather than merely tolerated, because a node's date is its preprint month and any later version of a paper may respond to anything already published. (2) The right number on the wrong quantity. Three times: reading VFT's @@Q_VFT_VFT@@ as a drop in reward hacking when it is the drop in unverbalized hacking; reading Alignment Faking's @@Q_AF_POSTRLHF@@ as a compliance gap when it is the alignment-faking reasoning rate; and reading BrowserART's @@Q_BART_CHAT@@ as a refusal rate when it is an attack-success rate — a sentence that printed the same metric with opposite verbs and, in doing so, softened the paper's actual finding. (3) A figure without the scope that makes it true. Also three times: "every baseline detector fell below @@Q_RM_AUROC@@" was true only on at least one subtask; the post-RLHF gap of @@Q_AF_POSTGAP@@ holds only on the training prompt and recovers to @@Q_AF_POSTGAP_RECOVERED@@ off it; GCG's @@Q_GCG_TRANSFER@@ transfer is specifically to GPT-3.5, where GPT-4 is @@Q_GCG_TRANSFER_GPT4@@. The second of those was introduced by the very sentence rewritten to fix class 2. (4) A claim wider than the number under it. Two figure captions asserted things their own charts refuted, and one universal quantifier was false on the rung its enumeration silently omitted; the captions are now tokens and the quantifier is scoped by a token counting the rungs it can range over.

The pattern across all three rounds is the finding worth keeping, and it is sharper than the caution it started as. Six of them were a number and a description coming apart — three wrong quantities, three missing scopes — and in every one of the six the taxonomy record was right and the prose wrapping the token was wrong. Not once did a generated figure drift; the sentences around them did, every time. So the fix in each case was structural rather than editorial: push the metric's name and the figure's scope into the paper's own key_number, and anchor the extraction regex through that label, so the build fails if the description and the number are ever separated again. A pipeline that computes its numbers has solved the easy half of the problem. The words attached to them are the half that still needs a guard.

Lineage. The @@N_EDGES@@ parent edges are relationships between papers inside this corpus (descends-from, responds-to, generalizes, formalizes, defends-against, measures, qualifies), not a full citation graph — @@N_RESPONDS_EDGES@@ of them are responds-to. The connection stories above use the graph to establish structure (who descends from whom, who never met) and the explainers to establish content; @@N_ROOTS@@ papers have no in-corpus parent and sit as roots.

The classification data lives in the atlas's data/failure-modes-taxonomy.json and the tags in papers.json; the page regenerates deterministically from both via python scripts/failure-modes_survey/build_survey_page.py.
