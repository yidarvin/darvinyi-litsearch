MedDialog: A Large-scale Medical Dialogue Dataset

Two crawled patient-doctor consultation corpora — MedDialog-CN and MedDialog-EN — assembled to give medical dialogue generation a real pretraining corpus and a transfer-learning testbed for low-resource clinical tasks. The reported size grew substantially between the arXiv preprint and the EMNLP 2020 camera-ready; this page is explicit about which number comes from which release.

// the gap

What problem is this solving?

Telemedicine promises cheaper, faster, more accessible care — the paper cites a Jefferson Health study claiming diverting patients from emergency departments to telemedicine can save over $1,500 per visit, and cites Pande and Morris (2015) showing telemedicine patients report lower depression/anxiety/stress and 38% fewer hospital admissions. But building a "virtual doctor" dialogue system that could plausibly triage or converse with patients needs a large, clean corpus of real doctor-patient conversation, and that data is hard to come by: it's privacy-sensitive, and prior public medical dialogue datasets were small and narrow. The paper's own comparison table names two: Muzhi (Wei et al., 2018), 710 dialogues covering 4 diseases, and Dxy (Xu et al., 2019), 527 dialogues covering 5 diseases — both built for a fairly different task, slot-filling / knowledge-graph-driven automatic diagnosis, not open-ended conversation. The same UCSD team's own COVID-19 dialogue sets (Yang et al., 2020) — CovidDialog-EN (603 dialogues) and CovidDialog-CN (1,088 dialogues) — illustrate the sharper version of the problem: whenever a new disease emerges, there is by definition almost no dialogue data about it yet, and a few hundred dialogues is nowhere near enough to train a sequence model from scratch.

MedDialog's pitch is to solve both problems with one resource: a corpus large and broad enough across specialties to pretrain a general medical dialogue model, plus a demonstration that pretraining on it can be transferred, via fine-tuning, to a low-resource target task like COVID-19 consultation — the paper's own stand-in for "the next disease we don't have data for yet."

// how it works

The method

MedDialog is built from two separately-crawled sources, each producing a dataset with a different internal structure. MedDialog-CN is crawled from haodf.com, a Chinese online consultation platform, with consultations spanning 2010–2020; each record has up to three parts — (1) a structured description of the patient's condition and history (present disease, duration, help needed, allergies, past medical history), (2) the multi-turn doctor–patient conversation itself, and (3) an optional diagnosis-and-treatment-suggestion block the doctor appends at the end. Consultations cover 29 broad specialty categories and 172 fine-grained specialties (cardiology, neurology, gastroenterology, urology, etc.). MedDialog-EN is crawled from two English-language platforms, iclinic.com and healthcaremagic.com, with consultations from 2008–2020; each record has only two parts — a short symptom description and the doctor–patient dialogue — with no separate diagnosis block, and covers 51 community categories and 96 specialties (andrology, cardiology, nephrology, pharmacology, etc.).

On top of the raw corpus, the EMNLP 2020 camera-ready (Zeng, Yang, et al., "MedDialog: Large-scale Medical Dialogue Datasets," ACL Anthology 2020.emnlp-main.743) adds a modeling study on MedDialog-CN, framing response generation two ways: as sequence-to-sequence learning, where a Transformer encoder–decoder (Vaswani et al., 2017) maps the conversation history s to the next doctor response t; and as language modeling, where a decoder-only GPT (DialoGPT-small architecture, 10 layers) generates t token-by-token conditioned on the concatenation of s and everything already generated. A third model, BERT-GPT, pretrains the encoder with BERT-style masked-token recovery and the decoder with GPT, then fine-tunes the pair on MedDialog-CN — the idea being that general-domain pretraining (on a separately assembled 15.4 GB Chinese corpus: 104M Wikipedia documents, 2.5M news articles from 63,000 sources, 4.1M community-QA documents, and 1.5M Baike-QA pairs) gives the model linguistic structure that fine-tuning on MedDialog-CN alone wouldn't teach it.

p(t|s) = p(t1|s) · ∏i=2n p(ti | s, t1, …, ti−1) // the language-modeling formulation of response generation used by GPT and BERT-GPT — s is the conversation history, t the doctor's response

MedDialog-CN is split 0.8:0.1:0.1 by dialogue (not by patient) into train/validation/test. Models are scored with perplexity, NIST-4, BLEU-2/4, METEOR, Entropy-4, and Dist-1/2 — a standard machine-translation-and-diversity toolkit the authors themselves flag as imperfect for dialogue (citing Liu et al., 2016, "How NOT to Evaluate Your Dialogue System"). To test transfer, the three MedDialog-CN-pretrained models are fine-tuned on the much smaller CovidDialog-Chinese set (1,088 dialogues, 9,494 utterances, 406,550 tokens) and compared against the same architectures trained on CovidDialog-Chinese alone, both by the automatic metrics above and by human ratings (5 annotators, 100 sampled dialogues, 1–5 scores for relevance, informativeness, and human-likeness).

// what they found

The results

Reported dataset size differs by release, and the two subsets should never be added together as one number. The EMNLP 2020 camera-ready's own statistics tables report MedDialog-CN at 3,407,494 dialogues, 11,260,564 utterances, 660,171,367 tokens across 172 specialties (Table 1), and MedDialog-EN at 257,332 dialogues, 514,664 utterances, 44,527,872 tokens across 96 specialties (Table 2). The arXiv v2 PDF's own body text (Section 2.2), by contrast, describes an earlier CN snapshot of only 1,145,231 consultations and 3,959,333 raw utterances (2,179,008 doctor + 1,780,325 patient; 3,209,660 after merging consecutive same-speaker turns) — yet that same v2 PDF's own comparison table (Table 1, page 4) already lists the larger 3,407,494 figure for MedDialog-CN, alongside Muzhi (710 dialogues/4 diseases), Dxy (527/5), COVID-EN (603/1), and COVID-CN (1,088/1). MedDialog-EN's count also shifts slightly across releases: v2's abstract, body text, and its own Table 1 agree at 257,454, while the EMNLP camera-ready's Tables 2 and 3 report 257,332 instead — a small decrease, and, unlike CN, one where v2 is internally self-consistent and the discrepancy only appears once you cross-check against the EMNLP version.

On the MedDialog-CN test set (EMNLP Table 5), BERT-GPT reaches the lowest (best) perplexity at 8.2, versus 9.5 for Transformer and 9.7 for GPT — attributed to BERT-GPT's general-domain pretraining. But on n-gram-overlap metrics BERT-GPT trails Transformer across the board — NIST-4 (0.31 vs 0.39), BLEU-2 (3.7% vs 4.9%), BLEU-4 (0.5% vs 0.9%), and METEOR (10.4% vs 13.1%) — while GPT edges out Transformer on both BLEU variants (BLEU-2 5.0%, BLEU-4 1.8%, the best of the three) despite scoring lower than Transformer on NIST-4 (0.36) and METEOR (12.1%). Diversity metrics (Entropy-4 ≈13.5–13.6, Dist-2 ≈2.0–2.1%) are essentially tied across models.

On the transfer task — fine-tuning MedDialog-CN-pretrained models on the 1,088-dialogue CovidDialog-Chinese set — pretraining improves all eight automatic metrics for Transformer (Table 8), 5 of 8 for GPT, and roughly half for BERT-GPT (the authors attribute the smaller gain for GPT/BERT-GPT to those models already being pretrained on other general-domain corpora, so MedDialog-CN pretraining adds less marginal value). Human evaluation (Table 9) is more uniformly positive: on all three model families, pretraining on MedDialog-CN raises relevance, informativeness, and human-likeness — e.g. GPT's human-likeness jumps from 1.80 (no pretrain) to 3.20 (pretrained), against a groundtruth ceiling of 3.78. Paired Student's t-tests (Table 10) put the pretrain-vs-no-pretrain difference at p<0.015 for 8 of 9 model/metric combinations, and p=0.031 for the ninth (BERT-GPT human-likeness).

// does it hold up?

The eval read

The most concrete eval-methodology problem with MedDialog is not a modeling choice — it's that the dataset's own headline size is internally inconsistent, and the inconsistency is easy to miss because it spans one document. The arXiv v2 PDF's body text (Section 2.2) reports MedDialog-CN at 1,145,231 dialogues, but the same PDF's own Table 1 two pages later reports 3,407,494 — a roughly 3× discrepancy inside a single release, only resolved by cross-checking against the EMNLP camera-ready's matching 3,407,494/11,260,564/660,171,367 statistics. A reader who cites "the MedDialog paper, 1.1 million conversations" (which is also what Semantic Scholar's cached abstract for the earlier v1 preprint still says) and a reader who cites "3.4 million" are both quoting this same paper, just different tables from different points in its revision history — and nothing in the text flags the jump or explains what changed (more crawling? deduplication reversed? a labeling error in one of the two passages?). For a dataset whose main selling point is scale, that ambiguity should be resolved before the number gets repeated uncritically downstream, which — given 253 citations on the arXiv record alone — it likely already has been.

The evaluation protocol has a second, more familiar issue: the paper is admirably candid that BLEU/METEOR/NIST-style n-gram metrics are unreliable for dialogue (citing Liu et al., 2016, by name), yet Table 5 — the only quantitative comparison on the full-scale MedDialog-CN benchmark — is built entirely from exactly those metrics plus perplexity, with no human evaluation on MedDialog-CN itself. Human evaluation only appears for the much smaller, 1,088-dialogue CovidDialog-Chinese transfer task, so the paper's strongest evidence (paired t-tests, p<0.04 across the board) applies to the transfer setup, not to the base MedDialog-CN benchmark that the modeling section is nominally about. There is also no reported correlation between the automatic metrics and human judgment on any shared set, so a reader can't tell whether BERT-GPT's lower perplexity but worse BLEU/METEOR (Table 5) means it is actually a better or worse medical dialogue generator — the paper's own metrics disagree with each other and there's no tiebreaker.

Because MedDialog is crawled, not authored or physician-reviewed, "doctor" turns are unverified real-world forum replies, not adjudicated clinical ground truth — visible directly in Figure 2's translated example, where the doctor's very first line ("The picture is not very clear") shows the diagnosis ("consider blepharitis") and the ointment prescription that follows were actually made against a photo the patient attached, not just the six labelled text fields (disease, description, help needed, duration, allergies, past history) that the released record contains. The stored dialogue is therefore not even a complete transcript of what the doctor was reacting to — an image the model, and any metric scoring against this "reference," never sees either. That's a realistic artifact of the source platforms, and arguably a feature for training conversational fluency, but it means every model in Table 5/8 is being scored against noisy, sometimes underspecified reference text, and no filtering, quality scoring, or physician audit of a reference subset is reported. The train/validation/test split is also purely dialogue-level and random (0.8:0.1:0.1) — there is no reported de-duplication by patient/user or by symptom-description text, so with 3.4 million dialogues crawled from a finite, decade-spanning pool of forum users, some amount of near-duplicate or same-user leakage across the split is plausible but unmeasured.

baselines

Transformer / GPT / BERT-GPT were the standard 2020 toolkit for conditional text generation and pretraining transfer; a fair comparison set for its era, though no retrieval/IR baseline is reported the way contemporary dialogue benchmarks (e.g. PERSONA-CHAT) included.

contamination

Split is random and dialogue-level, not by patient/user or de-duplicated by text similarity, so leakage across an 80/10/10 split of 3.4M crawled dialogues is plausible but unchecked; separately, years of public availability make MedDialog itself a near-certain pretraining-corpus contaminant for any LLM evaluated on it zero-shot after ~2021.

what's missed

No human evaluation or metric-correlation check on the full MedDialog-CN benchmark itself (only on the small CovidDialog-CN transfer task); no clinical-accuracy or physician-adjudication signal on the crawled "doctor" replies used as ground truth; the ~3× CN size discrepancy between the arXiv v2 body text and its own comparison table (and the EMNLP tables) is unflagged and unexplained.

would raise confidence

A single authoritative, versioned dataset-size table (with a changelog explaining any jump); a physician-audited quality/accuracy subset of MedDialog-CN; a patient/user-level split; and a human-eval or automatic-vs-human correlation study run directly on MedDialog-CN rather than only on the CovidDialog transfer task.

// bottom line

MedDialog's real contribution is scale and a genuinely useful transfer-learning demonstration — pretraining on it measurably helps a low-resource, novel-disease dialogue task on both automatic and human metrics. But the dataset's own reported size is internally inconsistent across (and even within) its two public releases, its evaluation candor about unreliable automatic metrics doesn't stop those metrics from carrying the main benchmark table, and its crawled, unaudited "doctor" ground truth means every number in this paper should be read as a fluency/engagement signal, not a clinical-accuracy one.

// takeaways

What to remember

  1. MedDialog pairs a Chinese corpus (haodf.com, 2010–2020) and an English corpus (iclinic.com + healthcaremagic.com, 2008–2020); the EMNLP 2020 camera-ready's final tables report 3,407,494 CN dialogues / 11,260,564 utterances / 660.2M tokens across 172 specialties, and 257,332 EN dialogues / 514,664 utterances / 44.5M tokens across 96 specialties — figures roughly 3× larger for CN than the 1,145,231 dialogues the arXiv v2 preprint's own body text describes.
  2. Pretraining Transformer, GPT, and BERT-GPT on MedDialog-CN and fine-tuning on the tiny 1,088-dialogue CovidDialog-Chinese set improves every automatic metric for Transformer, about half for GPT/BERT-GPT, and all three human-rated dimensions (relevance, informativeness, human-likeness) across all models, with paired-t-test p-values below 0.04 in every case tested.
  3. The dataset dwarfs earlier named medical dialogue sets by orders of magnitude (Muzhi: 710 dialogues/4 diseases; Dxy: 527/5) — but scale isn't clinical accuracy: "doctor" replies are unverified crawled forum text, and the paper reports no physician audit or clinical-correctness label for any of it.
  4. The authors flag, citing Liu et al. (2016), that BLEU/METEOR/NIST-style metrics are unreliable for dialogue evaluation — yet those are the only metrics reported for the full-scale MedDialog-CN benchmark; human evaluation and its significance testing appear only on the much smaller CovidDialog-Chinese transfer experiment.
