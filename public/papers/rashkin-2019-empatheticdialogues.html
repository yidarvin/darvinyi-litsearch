Towards Empathetic Open-domain Conversation Models: A New Benchmark and Dataset

EMPATHETICDIALOGUES: a 24,850-conversation, 32-emotion-label crowdsourced benchmark for whether a dialogue model can respond to someone's personal, emotionally-grounded story with something a human rates as empathetic — not just on-topic.

// the gap

What problem is this solving?

By 2018, the strongest open-domain dialogue architectures were trained on vast, barely-curated scrapes of internet text — Twitter, Reddit dumps, movie scripts, generic web text (the paper cites Ritter et al. 2010, Zhang et al. 2018, Mazaré et al. 2018, Devlin et al. 2018, and Radford et al. 2019 as this lineage). That training signal can bake in the aggressive, callous register that's common in spontaneous internet conversation, with no counterbalancing signal that teaches a model to recognize a conversation partner's feelings and acknowledge them — a skill the authors call empathetic responding. Chit-chat benchmarks existed (e.g. Dinan et al.'s ConvAI2, the Second Conversational Intelligence Challenge), but as of this paper the authors state that, to their knowledge, none specifically gauged whether a dialogue agent can converse with empathy.

The nearest existing resource with emotion labels, DAILYDIALOG (Li et al., 2017), falls short for this specific purpose on two counts: its ~13k dialogues are largely scripted, topic-driven scenarios aimed at English-language learners (ordering food, asking directions, introductions), and only about 5% of its utterances carry an emotion label other than "none" or "happy" — so the emotional signal is both thin and skewed. What was missing was a resource built around personal, first-person emotional situations, covering a deliberately broad and balanced span of emotions (not just a handful of basic ones), with an explicit single listener whose job is to respond to those feelings — which is exactly the gap EMPATHETICDIALOGUES (ED) is built to fill.

// how it works

The method

ED is collected in two crowdsourcing stages on Amazon Mechanical Turk via the ParlAI platform, using 810 US workers. First, a worker picks (or is assigned) one of 32 emotion labels — aggregated from several prior emotion-annotation schemas (Scherer & Wallbott 1994, Strapparava & Mihalcea 2007, Skerry & Saxe 2015, Li et al. 2017, Mohammad 2012) and spanning everything from "Surprised" and "Excited" to "Devastated" and "Ashamed" — and writes a 1–3 sentence situation description tied to that feeling (average 19.8 words). Second, that worker (the Speaker) is paired with another worker (the Listener) who has not seen the situation description or the emotion label, and the two have a 4–8-utterance conversation (average 4.31 utterances, average 15.2 words/utterance) about it — the Speaker describing what happened, the Listener responding based only on cues in the conversation itself. To keep the 32 labels from collapsing into a handful of popular ones, after the first few initial rounds of data collection workers doing the task for the first time were forced to pick one of the three emotion labels that had been chosen least often overall so far, while workers who had already done the task were instead offered the three labels they personally had chosen least often before; separately, workers were free to take as many HITs as they wanted for the first ~10k conversations, after which the more "frequently active" workers were capped at a maximum of 100 conversations each. The result is 24,850 conversations, split 19,533 / 2,770 / 2,547 into train/validation/test with no speaker's situation crossing a partition boundary, publicly released via ParlAI and GitHub.

Downstream, ED is used both as a training resource and as a benchmark: models are scored on how well they reproduce the Listener's side of held-out conversations, without ever seeing the emotion label or situation text (matching what the Listener saw during collection). Two base architectures are compared: a retrieval setup, where separate Transformer (or BERT) encoders embed the context and a candidate reply, and the model picks the candidate whose embedding has the highest dot product with the context embedding; and a generative setup, a full encoder–decoder Transformer trained to directly generate the reply token-by-token, with diverse beam search at inference.

All models are first pretrained on next-utterance prediction over a dump of 1.7 billion Reddit conversations, then either used as-is ("Pretrained"), fine-tuned on ED ("Fine-Tuned"), or — for the retrieval models — simply given ED's own training utterances as the pool of candidates to retrieve from at inference time, with no weight updates at all.

A third family of experiments asks whether a cheap, off-the-shelf classifier can add empathy signal without retraining the whole model. In the Prepend-1 setup, a pretrained classifier's single top-predicted label is prepended as a token to the input sequence before encoding — e.g. "I finally got promoted!" becomes "proud I finally got promoted!" — using either a fastText classifier trained to predict the ED emotion label from the Speaker's situation description (EmoPrepend-1) or a fastText classifier trained on the unrelated 20-Newsgroup topic-classification task (TopicPrepend-1), to test whether even topically-distant external supervision helps.

// what they found

The results

On automatic metrics (test set, Table 1), simply giving a pretrained retrieval model ED's own training utterances as its candidate pool — with no fine-tuning at all — raises AVG BLEU from 4.10 to 5.51 (plain Transformer) and from 4.26 to 5.97 (BERT), and the same candidate-pool swap lifts human-rated Empathy from 2.82 to 3.45 (Table 2). P@1,100 itself is a separate, fixed diagnostic, not a readout of that swap: it is accuracy at picking the true response out of 100 randomly-selected test-set candidates (the actual response always included among them), independent of which pool the model retrieves from during training or inference — which is why the paper reports it once per model rather than once per candidate source. By that measure, the Pretrained model scores 43.25 (plain Transformer) / 49.94 (BERT), and fine-tuning on ED pushes it further, to 56.90 (Transformer) and 65.92 (BERT); generative perplexity likewise drops from 27.96 (pretrained) to 21.24 (fine-tuned), with generative AVG BLEU rising from 5.01 to 6.27. The two Prepend variants land close to Fine-Tuned on these metrics and occasionally edge past it in a single column — EmoPrepend-1's BERT-retrieval P@1,100 (66.04) is marginally higher than Fine-Tuned's (65.92), and TopicPrepend-1's plain-retrieval AVG BLEU (6.00) edges out Fine-Tuned's (5.88) — but neither Prepend variant dominates Fine-Tuned across every automatic column, and on generative perplexity both Prepend variants (24.30, 25.40) are clearly worse than Fine-Tuned's 21.24.

Human ratings (Table 2; ≥100 ratings per model, 221 crowdworkers, Likert 1–5) tell a more consistent story specifically on Empathy. A pretrained generative model scores just 2.31 on Empathy; a pretrained retrieval model using Reddit candidates scores 2.82 (Transformer) / 3.06 (BERT). Fine-tuning on ED raises Empathy to 3.76 (plain retrieval, the best score in that architecture group) and 3.71 (BERT retrieval); the generative Fine-Tuned model reaches 3.25. Adding external supervision helps most for the higher-capacity BERT models: TopicPrepend-1 with BERT reaches Empathy 4.03 and Relevance 3.98 — the best scores in the BERT-retrieval group — while for the plain (non-BERT) Transformer retrieval group, Prepend does not beat Fine-Tuned on Empathy (TopicPrepend-1: 3.72 vs. Fine-Tuned's 3.76). Every model condition remains below the Gold Response ceiling of 4.19 Empathy / 4.55 Relevance / 4.68 Fluency — the score real human replies get when rated by the same task.

Fine-tuning on ED doesn't only help on ED itself: in a separate cross-corpus check (Table 6, Appendix C), fine-tuning improves next-utterance P@1,100 by 12–14% when evaluated on ED, by a smaller 5–7% when evaluated on DAILYDIALOG, and produces a 2–3% decrease when evaluated back on REDDIT — a trade-off the paper attributes to the pretrained model having been trained directly on Reddit prediction in the first place, and reads as encouraging evidence that ED fine-tuning partially generalizes rather than only overfitting to ED's own distribution.

// does it hold up?

The eval read

The central empirical claim — that fine-tuning on ED, or simply retrieving from its candidate pool, makes models rate as more empathetic — rests entirely on Likert human ratings with a reported standard error of about 0.1 per model condition (Table 3/9's caption). That means several of the paper's own "improvements" are on the edge of noise: Fine-Tuned's plain-retrieval Empathy (3.76) versus TopicPrepend-1's (3.72) is a 0.04-point gap against a ±0.1 SEM, well within the range where the two could plausibly tie. The paper reports no pairwise significance test (no bootstrap CI, no p-value) for any of these comparisons, so its narrative language ("improves," "boosts") is doing more work than the raw numbers alone can support for the smaller deltas — though the larger gaps (e.g. 2.31 → 3.76 Empathy, pretrained generative vs. fine-tuned retrieval) are not in serious doubt.

The 32-way emotion taxonomy itself is validated only in the appendix (Table 10, Appendix D.4), and the result is more ambiguous than the main text's confident framing suggests: reproducing Felbo et al. (2017)'s classification benchmark, the best classifier tested (a chain-thaw-finetuned DeepMoji) reaches only 0.48 accuracy predicting the emotion label from the Speaker's own situation description — and 0.45 on "ED-CUT," which re-runs the same test after removing every situation description that literally contains its own target emotion label word, isolating how much of that 0.48 came from the label word simply appearing verbatim in the text. A 32-way task with 0.48 accuracy is far above the roughly 3% random-chance floor, but it also means well over half the time even a strong classifier disagrees with the crowdworker-assigned label, consistent with the authors' own footnote that adjacent labels like "afraid" and "terrified" may be too close to reliably separate. Because Empathy ratings are reported per fine-grained emotion category in aggregate, this label noise is folded invisibly into the headline numbers rather than surfaced as a source of measurement uncertainty.

Baselines are reasonable for 2019 (Transformer retrieval, BERT, a full encoder–decoder generative Transformer, diverse beam search). One apparent metric-conflation in Table 1 turns out, on close reading of the automated-metrics passage (Sec. 5), not to be one: P@1,100 is always computed against the same fixed pool of 100 randomly-selected test-set candidates (the true response always included), regardless of the row's training/inference "Candidate Source," so the "-" cells on the Reddit-candidate rows just mean that model's P@1,100 was reported once, on its ED-candidate row, not that a harder or easier metric was silently swapped in. A related concern does survive one table over, though: the cross-corpus check in Table 6 (Appendix C) computes P@1,100 with both context and candidates drawn from that corpus's own pool — DailyDialog's 39.04 → 44.58 and Reddit's 58.95 → 56.25 are each measured against a topically distinct 100-candidate set drawn from a different corpus — yet the paper reads the resulting 12–14% (ED), 5–7% (DD), and −2–3% (R) deltas side by side as consistent evidence about how well ED fine-tuning "generalizes," without flagging that a percentage-point change in P@1,100 doesn't mean the same thing once the underlying candidate pool itself has changed.

Quality control for the crowdsourced conversations themselves is limited to "manually check[ing] random subsets of conversations by our most-frequent workers" (Section 3) — no inter-annotator agreement statistic (Krippendorff's alpha, Cohen's/Fleiss' kappa) is reported anywhere in the paper or its appendices for either the situation/emotion-label pairing or the human-rating task, which is a real gap for a dataset whose entire value proposition is the reliability of its emotion grounding.

baselines

Fair and current for 2019 (Transformer retrieval, BERT, full generative Transformer with diverse beam search); the Reddit-pretraining-then-fine-tune protocol is the standard recipe of the era.

contamination

Not an issue at publication (ED is a brand-new crowdsourced dataset with a clean train/val/test split by speaker). By 2026 standards, ED has been public on GitHub/ParlAI since 2019 and is a common fine-tuning target, so any modern LLM's zero-shot ED numbers should be read as likely pretraining-contaminated — a caveat this 2019 paper obviously couldn't anticipate.

what's missed

No inter-annotator agreement metric for the emotion-label/situation pairing or the human ratings; no significance testing on Likert deltas that are often within one reported SEM of each other; the 32-way emotion taxonomy's own ~0.48 classifier accuracy (Appendix D.4) suggests real label ambiguity that isn't reflected in how confidently the main results are framed.

would raise confidence

An agreement statistic for the emotion/situation collection step; paired significance tests (or confidence intervals) on the human-rating comparisons; and reporting Empathy broken out by how classifiable each emotion label is, so noisy labels (e.g. "afraid" vs. "terrified") don't silently blur the headline number.

// bottom line

EMPATHETICDIALOGUES's real contribution is turning "does this response sound like it cares" from an unmeasured vibe into a benchmark with a training set behind it — and the finding that even a cheap win — giving a pretrained model ED's own utterances to retrieve from, no fine-tuning required — meaningfully raises human Empathy ratings is genuinely useful and became a standard building block for later empathetic and long-term dialogue work. But the paper's own appendix undercuts the confidence of its main narrative: a ~0.48-accuracy emotion classifier and unreported human-rating significance mean the more nuanced ordering claims (Fine-Tuned vs. Prepend, e.g.) deserve more skepticism than the bolded table cells suggest.

// takeaways

What to remember

  1. EMPATHETICDIALOGUES pairs 24,850 crowdsourced conversations with 32 balanced emotion labels, each grounded in a Speaker's real personal situation, collected specifically so a Listener model can be scored on whether it acknowledges feelings rather than just staying on-topic.
  2. Simply giving a pretrained retrieval model ED's own training utterances as candidates — no fine-tuning — already lifts human-rated Empathy from 2.82 to 3.45 (plain Transformer); full fine-tuning pushes it to 3.76, and adding an external emotion/topic classifier's top label as a prepended token (TopicPrepend-1) reaches 4.03 for the higher-capacity BERT retrieval model, versus a 4.19 gold-response ceiling.
  3. Fine-tuning on ED generalizes only partially: it improves next-utterance retrieval on DailyDialog (+5–7% P@1,100) but slightly hurts it on the original Reddit pretraining distribution (-2–3%), a trade-off the authors disclose rather than hide.
  4. The paper's own appendix classification check finds only ~0.48 accuracy separating its 32 emotion labels — a real ambiguity in the label set that the main results don't explicitly correct for, and no inter-annotator agreement statistic is reported for either the label collection or the human ratings that carry the headline claims.
